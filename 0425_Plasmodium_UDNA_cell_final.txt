Graphical AbstractSummary (150 words)The pronounced genetic variability of the malaria parasite, Plasmodium falciparum is a key factor in its dominant presence worldwide. It provides the crucial antigenic variation, which helps in the evasion of the human immune system. Genomic modifications were shown to contribute to variability and accordingly, here we investigated genomic uracil patterns in Plasmodium falciparum. Uracil is a non-canonical base in the DNA and was previously shown to be present in significant amount in the P. falciparum genome. We first generated an inducible PfUNG-iKO strain, revealing the essential nature of uracil base excision repair. Our U-DNA-Seq results revealed structured enrichment of uracil in the variant surface antigen (VSA) gene families. Increased uracilation showed correlation with higher GC content and strongly overlaps with predicted G-quadruplex (G4) forming sequences, indicating the source of uracil to be from cytosine deamination in close proximity to G4s. The essential nature of PfUNG and the increased uracil in VSA families raises questions about a potential molecular mechanism to promote antigenic variation.Keywordsmalaria, antigenic variation, uracil, uracil DNA glycosylase, base excision repair, var gene, variant surface antigen, CRISPRIntroductionIn spite of the immense effort for decades to eradicate malaria worldwide, it still remains one of the major infectious diseases with an estimated 249 million cases in 2022. Following the health care disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, lethal malaria cases increased from 576.000 to 608.000 between 2019 and 20221. In addition, there has been a worrisome emergence of drug resistance to the currently used first line of defence artemisinin compounds 2,3. Despite the recent progress in vaccination, the lack of effective distribution and efficacy challenge the elimination of the disease4. Effective vaccination against the intraerythrocytic stages is limited by the ability of the parasite to generate and switch between antigenic variants during infection5,6. However, the underlying mechanisms driving genetic diversity in Plasmodium parasites remain elusive.Plasmodium falciparum - the major causative agent of severe malaria infections – has a genome with peculiarly high AT content, one of the most extreme among eukaryotes7. With approximately 70% AT content in genic and 90% in intergenic regions, it raises the question of what the reason for this skewed genomic composition is. It has been previously demonstrated in long-term in vitro culturing that parasites show an extreme mutational bias towards GC-to-AT transitions, which evolutionary could explain its elevated AT content8. The molecular mechanism of GC-to-AT mutations can be driven by oxidative or hydrolytic modification of DNA building bases. For instance, the oxidation of guanine to 8-oxo-guanine leads to pairing with adenine instead of cytosine, resulting in GC-to-TA transversion. Alternatively, the deamination of cytosine to uracil, leads to a pairing with adenine if left unrepaired, creating a GC-to-AT transition9. We have previously demonstrated that despite the presence of a functional DNA repair machinery10–13, the P. falciparum genome contains surprisingly high levels of uracil in all intraerythrocytic life cycle stages14, with around an order of magnitude higher genomic uracil content than human cancer cell lines such as HCT-11615. DNA repair pathways have been investigated due to their role in generating genetic diversity and facilitate the emergence of drug resistance16–19. Base excision repair (BER) is the primary pathway used to correct single base mutations. In case of genomic uracil, BER is initiated by the uracil DNA glycosylase enzyme family, which only has a single member encoded in the P. falciparum genome (PfUNG)14. The UNG enzyme cleaves the N-glycosylic bond and leaves an abasic site behind. The strand is then nicked by the apyrimidinic/apurinic endonuclease (ApnI) enzyme in the 5’ direction20,21. In mammalian cells, there are two ways in which the nicked strand can be repaired. Either a single nucleotide is exchanged in the so-called short-patch BER, or a longer nucleotide segment of up to 7 base pairs is replaced, referred to as long-patch BER22. It has been previously demonstrated that in P. falciparum BER predominantly occurs through long-patch BER due to the lack of DNA polymerase ?, which is required for the short-patch BER11,23. During long-patch BER, the damaged strand is replaced by the DNA polymerases ?/?, leaving a short single-stranded overhang called flap, which gets excised by the flap endonuclease (FEN). Finally, the single-stranded nick is ligated by the DNA Ligase I24. A prominent molecular mechanism in which uracil is used as a mediator to promote genetic diversity has already been widely established in B cell antibody diversification25–27. Somatic hypermutation (SHM) and class switch recombination (CSR) both rely on the introduction of uracil via the activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID) to trigger the generation of novel genotypes in an effort to establish a wider antibody repertoire28–30. UNG is triggered to excise the introduced uracils and the close proximity of multiple single-strand breaks from BER lead to double-strand breaks, which are primarily repaired via non-homologous end joining (NHEJ)29,31. The knockout of UNG abolishes CSR and SMH, hence its presence is crucial for the promotion of antibody diversification, albeit not essential for cellular viability32,33.Here, we characterize the impact of knocking out PfUNG, the initiator of uracil base excision repair. We utilized a dimerizable Cre-recombinase based P. falciparum strain34 to conditionally knockout PfUNG. Our observations indicate the essential nature of PfUNG, an interesting phenomenon as UNG is dispensable in most organisms35–38. Furthermore, we applied our U-DNA-Seq15 method to pull-down and determine uracilated genomic DNA fragments using whole genome sequencing (WGS). Our investigation revealed a notable and structured enrichment of uracil in the major variant surface antigen (VSA) gene families such as var and rif/stevor genes, indicating a prominent role of uracil in the genetic diversity of the malaria parasite. ResultsPfUNG-knock out parasites display a delayed lethal phenotypeFigure 1. RAPA-treated parasites showed a significant increase in their genomic uracil content after 88 hours of treatment (*p < 0.01, Welch’s t-test).To investigate the role of UNG in P. falciparum uracil BER, we generated a strain in which we replaced the endogenous ung locus with an HA-tagged version, flanked by loxP sites using a CRISPR-Cas9-based approach (Figure 1A). First, we assessed the generated HA-tagged strain in the absence of rapamycin (RAPA) to visualize the cellular localization of PfUNG by immunofluorescence assay (IFA). We also analyzed the distribution of uracilated DNA (U-DNA) in these parasites using a FLAG-tagged, catalytically inactive uracil DNA glycosylase protein as uracil sensor40. In ring stages, PfUNG expression is still low and U-DNA signal is not strong, but detectable. In trophozoite and schizont stages, we observed that U-DNA signal  overlaps with PfUNG signal and shows elevated levels across each nucleus (Figure 1B). We next characterized how the RAPA-induced excision of the PfUNG gene affected parasite growth and morphology. Gene excision was observed as early as 6 hours after RAPA addition in ring stages and the non-excised locus was undetectable after 24 hours of RAPA treatment (Figure 1C). Accordingly, the PfUNG protein expression which increased from trophozoite to schizont stage in control samples, was below the limit of detection following 24 hours of RAPA treatment  (Figure 1D). Subsequently, we monitored parasite growth using flow cytometry and microscopy over four intraerythrocytic cycles, in RAPA treated parasites (Figure 1E). Growth was normal in the first cycle, however by the end of the second cycle, a slight decrease in parasitemia was noted (8.6%±1.0% in RAPA-treated parasites, compared to 12.5%±2.9% parasitemia in DMSO treated parasites). Surprisingly, while RAPA treated parasites were able to progress through to the third cycle producing ring stage parasites which displayed WT morphology, these were unable to progress any further (Figure 1F). As PfUNG catalyzes the first step of genomic uracil removal, we hypothesised that its deletion would affect the genomic uracil content. Indeed, following quantification of U-DNA using a dot blot-based genomic quantification assay40, we saw that while there were no differences in the first cycle, U-DNA levels were significantly elevated by the end of the second cycle in iKO parasites (Figure 1G), confirming the role of PfUNG in uracil removal. Again, under both conditions a similar profile of enrichment was observed in VSA genes (Figure 2C). The pattern of uracilation was similar in DMSO and RAPA treated samples as indicated by a stronger correlation than the wild type 3D7 replicates (Figure S1B). However, peaks of uracilation were consistently higher, indicating more pronounced uracilation in the RAPA-treated U-DNA-Seq results (Figure S1D). We found a moderate correlation between log2(Uenrichment) and GC percentage (r = 0.42, Pearson’s correlation coefficient, Figure 4B), whereby the higher the GC content, the more uracilated those regions are. It has been previously reported that var genes are amongst the highest GC-percentage regions of the otherwise AT-rich genome of P. falciparum47. G-quadruplexes (G4s) are non-canonical DNA secondary structures that are formed in guanine-rich regions by four planarly aligned guanines on a single DNA strand coordinated by monovalent cations48. In P. falciparum, G4 have been identified and localised, and they have been shown to be present in the var gene loci47. To analyse the potential link between uracilation and G4 formation, we overlapped the predicted G4 forming genomic sequences available from a previous study47 with 10 bp long genomic intervals above a threshold of 0.5 in their log2(U-enrichment) value. Only well-defined predicted G4 forming sites with G4Hunter score above 1.2 and a size below 200 bp were considered to avoid oversampling due to broader prediction sites. Here, we identified high levels of uracil in the genome of P. falciparum and found that genes of variant surface antigens are particularly enriched in U-DNA. We propose that these high levels of uracil might contribute to the antigenic variation of the malaria parasite. Based on our previous observation of elevated genomic uracil levels across the different intraerythrocytic life cycle stages14, we suspected the contribution of uracil to the genetic diversity of P. falciparum. Therefore, we established a Cre-inducible PfUNG-iKO system using CRISPR-Cas9 to dissect the functional importance of the primary initiator of uracil BER. Our investigation reveals the essential nature of PfUNG in the intraerythrocytic life cycle of the parasite. This is a surprising result because UNG is mostly non-essential due to redundant pathways in higher eukaryotes such as mice49 and is completely absent in fruit fly36. Uracil excision repair in P. falciparum is entirely dependent on PfUNG with no presumed enzyme to fulfil the BER initiation role in its absence14. In Trypanosoma brucei, UNG is also the sole enzyme with uracil-DNA glycosylase activity, however its absence does not perturb cellular viability50,51.We used U-DNA-Seq to investigate the distribution of uracil in the genomic DNA of P. falciparum. Our analysis revealed a notable enrichment of uracil within the VSA gene families, most notably in the var gene family. Upon disrupting the endogenous PfUNG, the genomic uracil content slightly increased without any external perturbation. We further dissected the uracilation profile of each individual member within the var, rif and stevor gene families and the induced KO of PfUNG lead to a significantly increased uracilation in var genes. The U-DNA-Seq also revealed the structured enrichment of uracil along the first exon of var genes and along the second exon of rif/stevor genes. These genomic segments correspond to the part of the protein products, which gets exposed on the extracellular surface of red blood cells after export45,46. A few notable exceptions were the var3 genes and the var2csa, whose more conserved nature, hence less genetic diversity is in good correspondence with their lack of increased uracilation52,53.The observed structured uracilation raises the question what drives the specific enrichment of these genomic regions. Hence, we investigated what genomic features are enriched in highly uracilated segments. We found a correlation between the increased level of uracilation and higher GC content. Furthermore, we also show strong overlap between the most uracilated regions and the well-defined, high prediction score G4-forming sites. These observations might give an indication on the origin of uracil in P. falciparum DNA. If the increased uracilation is the result of erroneous dUTP incorporation instead of dTTP during replication, this would be expected to occur in AT-rich regions. However, it has already been shown that dUTP levels in P. falciparum are below the limit of detection and only elevated in the presence of dUTPase inhibitors54. These findings strongly indicate that the presence of uracil originates from cytosine deamination in the genomic DNA. It is further emphasized by the increase in genomic uracil content upon UNG KO, highlighting an internal mutational pressure that is present in the P. falciparum parasite.PfUNG is a close homolog of human and mouse UNG1 and UNG2 enzymes, nevertheless a notable difference in the sequence of PfUNG is the lack of a Replication protein A (RPA) binding site in comparison with the human isoforms (Figure S3). It has been previously demonstrated that the specific disruption of the RPA-binding site of UNG2 isoform in human and mouse B cells perturbs the elimination of uracil in the ssDNA context55,56. This functional difference could explain the higher prevalence of genomic uracil even in wild type 3D7 parasites. Here, we hypothesize a possible connection to G4 structures, more notably to the C-rich single stranded DNA opposite to these regions48. Cytosine deaminases show higher affinity toward ssDNA27,57,58,  and due to the lack of RPA-binding capability, PfUNG might leave uracil introduced into ssDNA unrepaired, leading to our observed uracil enrichment in close proximity to G4 structures.In summary, our results implicate the involvement of uracil in promoting genetic diversity of the VSA gene families, but the molecular mechanism remains elusive. We hypothesize that U-DNA would emerge through cytosine deamination. Our preliminary homology search analyses did not reveal a strong cytidine deaminase candidate, except for presumed ADAT2/3 homologues (PF3D7_1349400, PF3D7_1204600). This group of enzymes has been previously shown to be able to act as a cytidine deaminase in T. brucei and amphioxus, albeit in ssDNA context59,60. We suggest that the presence of U-DNA could significantly contribute to the genomic diversity of the parasite in both its antigenic diversity and its genomic diversity in general.
STAR MethodsParasite CulturePlasmodium falciparum 3D7, p230 and PfUNG-iKO strains were cultured in A+ erythrocytes (Hungarian National Blood Transfusion Services, Budapest, Hungary) in complete malaria culture medium (CM) of RPMI 1640 with 25 mM of HEPES and L-glutamine  (VWR Chemicals, Radnor, PA, USA) supplemented with 37 µM hypoxanthine (VWR Chemicals, Radnor, PA, USA), 5 g/L Albumax I (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and 25 mg/L gentamycin (Gibco from Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Cultures were maintained at 5% hematocrit, 37 °C in an atmosphere of 5% O2, 5% CO2, and 90% N2. For transfections, we supplemented the media with 10% human serum instead of Albumax I. Parasites were synchronized using both the Percoll- and sorbitol-based synchronization methods for late schizont and ring stage parasites, respectively. Briefly, parasite pellets containing late stage schizont were layered on top of 70% Percoll (Sigma-Aldrich, Burlington, MA, USA) in RPMI 1640 and centrifuged for 11 minutes at 1,300g. Red blood cells infected with late schizonts were recovered and washed twice with CM. For the sorbitol synchronization, we used 5 volumes of 5% sorbitol (Sigma-Aldrich, Burlington, MA, USA) solution per cell pellet volume on high parasitemia ring cultures. Parasites were incubated in the sorbitol solution for 10 minutes at 37°C and washed twice using CM.UNG transgenic plasmid constructWe designed a UNG construct to be integrated into the ung locus on chromosome 14 of P. falciparum. The construct was designed for the conditional knockout system mediated by a parasite expressing a dimerizable Cre-recombinase34. One loxP site in an artificial intron39 was inserted in the ung gene at position 334 with respect to the start codon. The second loxP was present downstream of the STOP codon. The homology regions (HR) were amplified from P. falciparum 3D7 genomic DNA, using primers p1+p2 for HR1 (chr. 14 – 607,015:607,533) that includes the first 334bp of the ung gene, and primers p3+p4 for HR2 (chr. 14 – 605,838:606,432) containing 595bp downstream of the STOP codon of the ung locus. The second part of the ung gene, containing the last 676 bp of the gene, was recodonized to avoid subsequent recognition by the guide RNAs. The recodonized gene fragment has been ordered by gene synthesis (Integrated DNA Technology, IDT, USA). Finally, the two fragments were cloned into the pLN-3HA plasmid using Infusion HD Cloning Kit (Takara Bio, USA). The pLN-3HA plasmid contains a 3x-hemagglutinin-tag (HA-tag) downstream of the multi cloning site, which fuses to the C-terminal end of the PfUNG protein. One clone was selected for all analyses.Growth assayPfUNG-iKO parasites were tested for fitness and viability phenotype. Prior to induced knockout, we Percoll-synchronized late-stage parasites and subsequently sorbitol-synchronized them 6 hours later to obtain a short time window parasite culture. After the double synchronization, we set the parasitemia to 1% at 5% hematocrit and induced the conditional knockout of the ung gene by the addition of 50 nM rapamycin (Merck, Germany). Genomic DNA was isolated from 1 ml of Percoll and sorbitolsynchronized high parasitemia culture using the ZymoGene Quick-DNA MiniPrep Plus Kit and fragmented using a Diagenode Bioruptor Plus to generate 200-400 bp long fragments for library preparation. U-DNA immunoprecipitation relies on the 3x-FLAG-tagged ∆UNG construct used for the aforementioned dot blot-based genomic uracil quantification assay and was carried out as described previously15. Briefly, we used antiflag agarose beads (A2220, Sigma-Aldrich, Burlington, MA, USA) and incubated the 3x-FLAG-∆UNG sensor overnight. We then incubated two-third of the fragmented DNA samples with the equilibrated beads for 3 hours, while we kept one-third as INPUT sample for the whole genome sequencing. We eluted the DNA from the beads using 1% SDS and 0.1M NaHCO3 by vortexing for 5 minutes, repeated twice with an additional incubation for 20 minutes. Finally, to remove leftover RNA and protein contamination, we treated the samples with 150 µg/ml of RNase A (Macherey- Nagel, Düren, Germany) and 500 µg/ml of Proteinase K (Zymo Research, Orange, CA, USA) for an hour each at 37°C. We purified the eluted DNA with the Macherey-Nagel NucleoSpin Gel and PCR Clean-up Kit and measured the concentration of the samples using QuBit dsDNA HS Quantification Kit and a QuBit 3 Fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).Whole genome sequencing and data analysisSamples were sent for whole genome sequencing to iBioScience (Pécs, Hungary). Samples underwent quality control to confirm their quality for sequencing. The libraries for Illumina sequencing were prepared using NEBNext Ultra II DNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB, Ipswitch, MA, USA). Briefly, 10 ng DNA was end prepped, adapter ligated and purified. Finally, the libraries were amplified according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The quality of the library was checked on 4200 TapeSation System using D1000 Screen Tape (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA, USA), and the quantity was measured on Qubit 3.0 (Thermo Fisher, Waltham, MA, USA). Illumina sequencing was performed on a NovaSeq 6000 instrument (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) with 2x151 run configuration. Raw data were obtained and quality checked using FastQC and multiQC. For all samples, the reads were mapped to the Plasmodium falciparum 3D7 reference genome (version 57, obtained from plasmodb.org63) using the Burrow-Wheeler Alignment bwa mem mapping. Aligned reads were converted to bam files, which were then sorted and indexed using samtools. Duplicate reads were marked and removed using picard-tools. We calculated the genomic content normalized coverage of all samples using the bamCoverage package of the deepTools suite42, which resulted in genome-scaled bigWig coverage tracks. Finally, for each sample, we compared the genome-scaled coverage tracks of the immunoprecipitated (U-DNA-IP) and input datasets to determine log2 enrichment using bigwigCompare of the deepTools suite. Due to the repetitive nature of telomere regions, these were blacklisted from all downstream analysis.Gene ontology enrichment analysis was carried out by averaging the log2 enrichment for each 5318 protein coding genes using bedtools64 and submitting the top 200 genes to ShinyGO65 (Table S1). After the identification of the enrichment in VSA genes, we extracted the sequences for var, rifin and stevor genes and statistically analysed their log2(U-enrichment) in RStudio using Welch’s ttest. Heatmap of the enrichment along the var and rif/stevor genes was generated using computeMatrix and plotHeatmap in the deepTools package42. Ideogram has been generated using the RIdeogram66 package, VSA gene coordinates have been collected from PlasmoDB63. Distance to predicted G4 sites were calculated with bedtools using 10 bp bin size merged data of bins with a log2(U-enrichment) above a threshold of 0.5, corresponding to a 40% increase in likelihood of uracilation.
